Want to donate your old sofa or wardrobe to charity? A simple guide to getting it there

Giving good furniture to charity is one of the nicest ways to clear space. A bed, a sofa or a washing machine you no longer need can help furnish someone's first home or raise money for a cause you care about. Here's how to make it go smoothly.

1. Check the item will be accepted.

Charities can only take things they can safely pass on or sell. Upholstered furniture, such as sofas, mattresses and padded chairs, usually needs its fire safety label still attached. Items should be clean and free of rips or stains, and electricals should work. Every charity has its own list, so look at their website or ring them before you plan anything.

2. Ask about their collection service first.

Many charity furniture shops, hospices and furniture banks collect donated furniture for free. Book early, because slots can fill up at busy times of year.

3. If they can't collect, ask about dropping it off.

Some furniture banks only take drop-offs, at set times. Write down the opening times and where to unload.

4. Measure, and think about access.

Note the sizes of the bigger pieces, which floor they're on, and any tight stairs or doorways. That helps whoever moves it.

5. If you can't get it there yourself.

If the charity can't collect when you need it and you don't have a suitable vehicle, you can post the drop-off free on Pink Pumpkin Logistics. Local drivers quote to take it to the charity during its opening times, and you pick the one you're happy with. (Full disclosure: it's our site, based in the East Midlands around Nottingham NG1.) Have the charity confirm it'll accept the item first, because we move things and can't take away anything that's turned down.

6. Don't forget Gift Aid.

Some charities can claim Gift Aid on donated goods when they're sold, so ask whether they'd like you to fill in a form.

Your old furniture gets a second life, someone else gets a home that feels more like home, and you get your space back. Everybody wins.
