Found the perfect second-hand piece at the other end of the country? How to get it home without the 600-mile round trip

It happens all the time. You've been searching for weeks for the right dresser, sofa, piano or set of chairs, and there it is, at a good price, in perfect condition… and collection only, five hours up the motorway. Or your daughter has just moved into her first flat at the other end of the UK and you've got a spare bed and a wardrobe she'd love. Here's how to get things like that from A to B without losing a whole weekend to driving.

1. Check it before you commit

Ask the seller for a few extra photos: close-ups of any marks, the back and underneath, and one with a tape measure across it. Measure your own doorways, stairs and the spot it's going into. When it's a long way off, you only want to arrange it once.

2. Agree the collection details with the seller

Settle on payment, a collection window that suits them and how it's being handed over. Say a driver will be collecting rather than you, swap phone numbers, and ask whether it's on the ground floor or upstairs and whether there's parking outside.

3. Be flexible on dates if you can

Long-distance jobs are easier to fit in when the dates aren't fixed to one day. A driver who's already doing that route may be able to quote for it. If it has to arrive by a certain day, for a birthday or Christmas, say so up front.

4. Getting it home

You can post the job free on Pink Pumpkin Logistics (https://pinkpumpkinlogistics.co.uk), a UK site where drivers send quotes and you choose the one you're happy with. There's no obligation to accept. Give the pickup and drop-off towns, what the item is, its rough size and weight, any stairs at either end, whether it needs two people to carry, and how flexible your dates are.

5. On the day

Let the seller know the driver's name and rough arrival window, keep your phone on, and have the space cleared at your end. Check it over when it arrives, then enjoy the bit you've been waiting for: seeing it in its new home.
