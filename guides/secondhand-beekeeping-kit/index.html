Starting beekeeping with second-hand kit? Here's a calm way to get it home

Lots of new beekeepers start with kit from a retiring beekeeper, a family clear-out or an association sale. Hives, supers, stands and extractors are bulky, oddly shaped and often stacked in a shed at the end of a garden. Here's how to get yours home without a fuss.

1. Empty kit only

Make sure everything you're collecting is empty: no bees, no colonies, no frames with brood. Moving live colonies is a beekeeper's job, not a transport job.

2. Ask the seller to clean and stack it

Ask for boxes, roofs and floors to be scraped and stacked together, frames bundled, and the extractor drained and dry. Clean kit is lighter, tidier and nicer to load.

3. Make a simple list

How many hive bodies, supers, roofs, floors and stands, plus any extractor, settling tank or tools. A list helps you check nothing is left behind and helps a driver judge the space.

4. Protect the moving parts

Strap extractor lids shut, tape taps closed, and wrap the drum so it doesn't dent. Tie frames into bundles so they can't slide about.

5. Think about access

Kit is often at the bottom of a garden or on an allotment. Note any gates, steps, muddy paths and where a van can park.

6. Describe the job clearly

What's being moved, roughly how much, where it's collected from and going to, access at both ends, and when the seller needs it gone.

8. Get set up for spring

Once it's home, store it dry and off the ground, check it over through the winter, and you'll be ready when the season starts.
